# Ingredients - Makes 16 to 18 four-ounce foil ramekins, filled ¾ full (about 95-100 g each)

## Mushrooms
* 1.5 lbs. cremini (baby bella) mushrooms, about 9 cups, sliced 3 mm thick
* 3 tbsp. neutral high-heat oil (canola, avocado, vegetable), split into two 1.5 tbsp. batches
* ¾ tsp. kosher salt, split between the two batches

## Custard
* 3 cans Aroy-D coconut milk, 14 fl. oz. each. Chill 2 hours before opening.
  * Skim ¾ cup (180 g) thick cream off the top for the cap
  * Measure 3 ¾ cups (900 g) of the rest for the custard
* 4 tbsp. (65 g) MaeSri red curry paste, plus a tiny dab for the red swirl
* 1 tbsp. (15 g) MaeSri chili paste with sweet basil leaves. This is the zero-chop basil shortcut.
* 4 whole makrut (kaffir) lime leaves, steeped whole and strained out
* 6 whole eggs + 3 yolks (300 g + 54 g), room temperature
* 3 tbsp. (45 g) fish sauce
* 1 tbsp. (15 g) low-sodium soy sauce. Dialed back for the MaeSri paste, which is much saltier than Thai Kitchen.
* 3 tbsp. packed (36 g) light brown sugar

## Cream Cap
* ¾ cup (180 g) skimmed coconut cream
* ¼ tsp. kosher salt

## Dippers
* 3 packs Bin Bin rice crackers (105 g each)
* 1 tub (8.8 oz) 3 Mien fried shallots, for sprinkling

## Gear
* 16-18 four-ounce disposable aluminum foil ramekins with lids
* 1-2 deep disposable aluminum roasting pans for the water bath, plus heavy-duty foil
* Mandoline with a straight slicer insert and hand guard (or a cut-resistant glove)
* 12-inch skillet
* Immersion blender, deep bowl or tall pitcher, fine-mesh strainer

# Recipe

## Prep
* Preheat oven to 325°F (163°C), rack in the center. Put a kettle of water on to simmer.
* Open the chilled coconut milk cans without shaking. Spoon the solid top cream into a small bowl until you have ¾ cup (180 g). Whisk in the ¼ tsp. salt and set aside. Measure the remaining 3 ¾ cups (900 g) for the custard.
* Set the foil ramekins inside the roasting pan(s) on the counter.
* Slice all the mushrooms 3 mm thick on the mandoline. Use the hand guard here. 1.5 lbs. is a lot of slicing and this is the only fiddly part of the recipe.

## Mushrooms
* Heat half the oil in the 12-inch skillet over medium-high until shimmering.
* Add half the mushrooms and half the salt in an even layer. Sear undisturbed for 2 minutes, then stir occasionally for 3-4 minutes, until every drop of liquid has cooked off and the edges are deeply golden brown.
* Transfer to a bowl and repeat with the remaining oil, mushrooms, and salt.
* Divide the intact mushrooms evenly among the foil cups. Don't press or chop them. Pressing squeezes water back out, which is the whole thing you're trying to avoid.

## Curry Base
* Drop the skillet to medium-low. Add the red curry paste and the sweet basil chili paste and fry for 60 seconds, stirring constantly, until fragrant.
* Pour in the 900 g of coconut milk and drop in the 4 lime leaves. Whisk hard, scraping up every bit of caramelized paste. You're aiming for smooth and warm, about 110-120°F (43-49°C). Don't simmer it, just dissolve the paste and melt the fat so it emulsifies.
* Pull off the heat.

## Custard
* In a deep bowl or tall pitcher, whisk the whole eggs, yolks, fish sauce, soy sauce, and brown sugar until smooth.
* Ladle about 1 cup of the warm coconut-curry liquid into the eggs while whisking to temper, then pour in the rest, lime leaves and all.
* Submerge the immersion blender all the way to the bottom and give it 4-5 short pulses on low speed. Do not run it continuously, or you'll whip air in and get a spongy custard.
* Pour through the fine-mesh strainer into a clean pitcher. This catches the lime leaves, any paste lumps, and any stray bits of egg.

## Bake
* Pour the custard over the mushrooms, filling each cup ¾ full.
* Dollop 1 tsp. of the salted coconut cream onto the center of each cup. Dip a toothpick in the raw red curry paste and swirl a pinpoint of red into each white dollop.
* Slide the pan onto the oven rack, then pour simmering water into the pan until it reaches halfway up the sides of the cups.
* Lay foil loosely over the top, offset to one side so condensation drips into the water bath instead of onto the custards.
* Bake 18-22 minutes, checking at 18.

## Cool and Store
* Turn the oven off, crack the door 2 inches, and leave the cups in the warm water bath for 10 minutes. This is the step that prevents thermal shock, sinking, and weeping.
* Move the cups to a kitchen towel and cool completely, 30-40 minutes.
* Snap on the lids and refrigerate up to 24 hours.

## Serve
* Pull half the batch 45-60 minutes before guests arrive, lids on, so the tops stay glossy while they come up to temperature.
* Lid off right before serving. Room temperature, never cold straight from the fridge.
* Bin Bin crackers alongside, plus a small bowl of fried shallots for sprinkling.
* Swap in the second wave from the fridge halfway through the party so nothing sits out past the 2-hour window.

# Doneness - Check at 18 minutes

* **Surface:** flat to barely convex. If it domes high, pull it immediately. Doming is what happens right before it cracks.
* **Rim:** zero gap between the custard and the foil cup wall. A visible gap means it's overbaked.
* **Jiggle:** nudge the pan. The outer ¾ inch should be matte and set while a quarter-sized spot in the center still wobbles like soft gelatin.
* **Knife and temp:** center reads 170-172°F (77-78°C), and a paring knife tip comes out coated in creamy custard, neither watery nor bone dry.

# Notes

* The two ways to ruin this are an overbaked custard and a cold one. The bake is short and the margin is narrow, so use a thermometer the first time through.
* MaeSri paste is considerably saltier and hotter than the supermarket brands. The soy sauce is already reduced to compensate. If you swap pastes, taste the custard base before it goes in the oven and adjust.
* The sweet basil chili paste does the job of fresh Thai basil with no chopping. If you have fresh basil, chiffonade it over the tops after baking instead. Basil baked under a custard for 20 minutes goes dark and limp.
* The cream cap is what makes it look like the restaurant version, and it costs nothing. Skim it before you shake the cans, otherwise it disappears into the custard.
